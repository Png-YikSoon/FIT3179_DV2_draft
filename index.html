FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · Domestic air travel in Australia

Up in the Air

Australia is a country the size of Europe with most of its people in a handful of coastal cities. For many trips, flying is the only practical way to get around. How many people fly, where do they go, and why?

01

When Australians fly

A Nation in Motion: Domestic Passenger Trends, 2014–2026

DRAFT: About 61 million passengers flew in 2019. In 2020, border closures cut this to 19 million. By 2025, traffic was back to 60 million, about 98% of the 2019 level.

Australia's Travel Calendar: When Do Passengers Fly?

Each ring is one year (2015 in the centre, 2025 on the outside). Each segment is a month. Blue months were busier than that year's average month; red months were quieter.

DRAFT: point out the spring peak (October), the January holiday rush and the quiet February.

Holiday Peaks or Everyday Journeys?

Each line shows a route's average month from 2023 to 2025. 100 means a typical month for that route. DRAFT: leisure routes swing with the holidays; business and mining routes stay steadier.

The busiest routes

Routes Rewritten: Which Connections Recovered?

Passengers on 2019's 15 busiest routes, compared with 2025. The scale is logarithmic, so equal slopes mean equal percentage changes.

DRAFT: Melbourne–Sydney is still number one but carried 12% fewer passengers; Brisbane–Perth grew 19%.

How Full Are the Planes? Demand Across Routes and Months

The "load factor" is the share of seats that had a paying passenger. Darker squares mean fuller planes. July 2025 to June 2026.
