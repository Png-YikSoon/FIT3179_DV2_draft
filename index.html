FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · Domestic air travel in Australia

Up in the Air

Australia is about 23 times the size of Malaysia, yet most of its 27 million people live in a handful of cities on the coast. For many journeys, flying is the only practical way to get around. How many Australians fly, where do they go, and why?

01

When Australians fly

A Nation in Motion: Domestic Passenger Trends, 2014–2026

Passengers on domestic flights each month (thin line) and the 12-month average (thick line).

Key findings

A 97% collapse

In April 2020 only 146,000 people flew, down from 5.2 million in April 2019, as states closed their borders.

Almost back

60.4 million passengers flew in 2025, 98% of the 2019 record of 61.4 million.

Fuller planes

Airlines filled 82.4% of seats in 2025, up from 80.7% in 2019.

Australia's Travel Calendar: When Do Passengers Fly?

Each ring is a year (2015 in the centre, 2025 outside); each segment is a month. Blue months were busier than that year's average month, red months quieter.

Key findings

October is the peak

In normal years, October is 9% busier than an average month, lining up with the spring school holidays.

A winter escape in July

July is 6% above average, during the winter school holidays.

February is the quietest

February sits 12% below average: it is the shortest month and comes straight after the summer holidays.

Averages exclude 2020–2022, when lockdowns, not seasons, decided when people flew.

Holiday Peaks or Everyday Journeys?

Average passengers in each month, 2023–2025, on eight routes. 100 means a typical month for that route.

Key findings

Melbourne–Sydney barely changes

Its busiest and quietest months are only 17 points apart: people fly it all year round.

Cairns peaks in July

Southerners head to the warm tropics in winter: July is 24% above average, February 29% below.

Canberra empties in January

Canberra–Sydney drops 33% below average in January, when the capital's government offices slow down for summer.

Mining routes stay steady

Perth–Port Hedland stays within 12% of its average all year, as fly-in fly-out (FIFO) workers travel on fixed rosters.

02

Where the planes go

Connected by Air: Australia's Domestic Flight Network

62 major routes, July 2025 to June 2026. Thicker lines carried more passengers. Hover over a line to see the route.

Key findings

One route, one in seven passengers

Melbourne–Sydney carried 8.1 million passengers, about 22,000 a day and 15% of all passengers on these routes.

The east-coast triangle

Flights linking Sydney, Melbourne and Brisbane carry 30% of all passengers.

Crossing a continent

Brisbane–Perth is 3,615 km, longer than Kuala Lumpur to Hong Kong (about 2,500 km), but still a domestic flight.

Perth is the west's hub

Every Western Australian regional route in the data connects through Perth.

Australia's Aviation Hubs: Where Traffic Concentrates

Circle size shows passengers on major routes through each airport (2025–26). Colour compares traffic with 2018–19, before COVID-19: blue is busier, red is quieter.

Key findings

Mining towns boomed

Karratha +61%, Kalgoorlie +54%, Newman +39% and Port Hedland +38%: all home to FIFO mining workers.

The big cities lag

Sydney (−11%) and Canberra (−12%) are still below 2018–19, in line with the drop in business travel (Section 04).

The Red Centre has not recovered

Alice Springs is down 59% and Uluru (Ayers Rock) 38%.

Fewer than half are back

Only 18 of 38 airports handle more passengers than before the pandemic.

03

The busiest routes

The Race for the Top: How Australia's Busiest Routes Change

Top 10 routes by passengers in each financial year (July to June, so "2025–26" means July 2025 to June 2026). Hover over a line to follow one route.

Key findings

Melbourne–Sydney never loses

It ranked first every year, even in 2020–21 when it carried just 1.7 million passengers.

Cairns' lockdown leap

Brisbane–Cairns jumped to 3rd in 2020–21: with state borders shut, Queenslanders could still fly within their own state.

The Gold Coast climbs

Melbourne–Gold Coast rose from 9th to 6th since 2014–15.

Routes Rewritten: Which Connections Recovered?

Passengers on 2019's 15 busiest routes, compared with 2025. The scale is logarithmic, so equal slopes mean equal percentage changes.

Key findings

Only 6 of 15 recovered

Together these routes carried 5% fewer passengers in 2025 than in 2019.

Business routes lost most

Canberra–Melbourne −17%, Melbourne–Sydney −12% and Brisbane–Sydney −9%.

Brisbane–Perth is the winner

Up 19%, the strongest growth of any top route.

How Full Are the Planes? Demand Across Routes and Months

Load factor (the share of seats with a paying passenger) on the 15 busiest routes, July 2025 to June 2026. Darker squares mean fuller planes.

Key findings

Gold Coast flights are fullest

Melbourne–Gold Coast averaged 91% of seats filled and passed 90% in 8 of 12 months.

October fills up

The fullest month across these routes (88%); February is the emptiest (82%).

Most empty seats

Canberra–Melbourne averaged only 76%.

04

Why Australians travel

The charts in this section use Tourism Research Australia's survey of all domestic trips, by any kind of transport (car, bus, train or plane), not just flights.

Work or Escape? Why Australians Travel Domestically

Main reason for trips, year ending June 2026. Each square is 1% of trips.

Key findings

Half of all trips are holidays

50% of trips, or about 201 million a year.

Family comes second

Visiting friends and relatives accounts for one in three trips and 38% of overnight trips.

Business is a small slice

Only 7% of all trips and 8% of overnight trips.

Is the Purpose of Travel Changing?

Trips in each quarter compared with the June quarter 2025 (= 100).

Key findings

Business travel fell 18%

Trips dropped from 8.7 million to 7.1 million a quarter in one year. Overnight business trips fell 38%.

Holidays grew 9%

Holiday trips rose from 46.8 million to 50.8 million a quarter.

Spending on flights

Australians spent $4.5 billion on domestic airfares in the June quarter 2026. Airfare spending on day trips more than doubled in a year.

Domestic Travel Across Australia: Where Tourism Is Growing

Change by state visited, year ending June 2026 compared with the year before. Use the menu to switch measures. * Smaller states and territories have higher survey error.

Key findings

Every state had more trips

Total trips grew everywhere, led by Queensland (+7.7%) and Western Australia (+7.3%).

Overnight stays held steady

Overnight trips changed by less than 2% in every state: the growth came from day trips.

Spending tells a different story

Overnight spending rose 22% in the Northern Territory and 12% in Western Australia, but fell 13% in the ACT.

Short Break or Long Holiday?

Share of holiday trips by length, for travel groups with and without children, June quarter 2026.

Key findings

Two or three nights is the norm

About half of holidays for both groups (50% with children, 48% without).

Families stay longer

27% of family holidays lasted four to seven nights, compared with 21% for groups without children.

More one-night trips without children

23% compared with 17% for families.
